Te Araroa (The Long Pathway) is a continuous 3,000km trail which spans the entire length of New Zealand, from Cape Reinga in the north to Bluff in the south.

The route links hiking tracks, farmland, quiet roads, beaches and one river journey. Heading south, it starts with Ninety Mile Beach and works through Northland's forests and coast. Next comes Auckland, which you cross on foot, then the farmland and riverside paths of the Waikato. The Central North Island follows, with Pirongia Maunga and the volcanic country around Tongariro, before the only paddling section: roughly 100km down the Whanganui River by kayak or canoe. The North Island ends in the Tararua Range, a short but exposed and notoriously weather-prone stretch above Wellington, where you take the ferry to the South Island. There, the trail crosses the Richmond Range and the Nelson Lakes, including Waiau Pass, the second highest point on the trail. Next are Canterbury's big river valleys and Harper Pass, then Stag Saddle at 1,925m, the highest point on the whole route. It finishes through Otago's high country and Southland's remote southern tramping, ending at Bluff.

The North Island makes up a little over half the distance. A complete walk usually takes 3 to 5 months, so most people start in spring and aim to finish before winter. Logistics are mostly self-supported: resupply happens in towns along the way, with DOC huts and campsites linking the wilder sections.
